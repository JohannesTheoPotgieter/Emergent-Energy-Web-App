Executive Overview
==================
This playbook controls how Emergent delivers C&I solar PV and BESS
projects from first contact with a prospect to long-term operations. It
is the single source of truth for our lifecycle, our stage gates, our
ownership, and the evidence we expect at every handover.
It exists because we have learned --- sometimes painfully --- that an
operating model that lives in people\'s heads is not an operating model.
Process, people, and tools must reconcile. Pipedrive, SharePoint, the
app, QuickBooks, and Excel must tell the same story about every project.
Where they don\'t, we lose money, time, or both.
How to read this document
-------------------------
-   Each lifecycle stage opens with a stage-gate block: stage, purpose,
    inputs, exit gate, accountable, responsible, tools.
-   Each stage closes with a gate the project must pass to move to the
    next stage. No stage movement without evidence.
-   Industry standards and regulatory facts are stated where they apply.
    They are not optional.
-   Companion templates (referenced throughout) live in the SharePoint
    Templates library and are tracked in the app against each project.

Lifecycle Stage 1: First Assessment
===================================
*Decide whether the opportunity is worth design effort. Before a cent is
spent on engineering, the EPC must qualify.*
  **Stage**         First Assessment
  ----------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Purpose**       Confirm whether the opportunity is worth design effort.
  **Inputs**        Pipedrive deal, utility bills (12 months), site details, client decision-maker, basic load profile.
  **Exit gate**     Opportunity accepted or rejected for Cost Proposal & Design.
  **Accountable**   Head of Project Development.
  **Responsible**   Project Developer.
  **Consulted**     Engineering (technical sniff test), Finance (commercial fit), Compliance (red-flag check).
  **Tools**         Pipedrive, SharePoint opportunity folder, app opportunity record.
  **Output**        Pursue / Decline decision logged in Pipedrive and the app, with reason.
1.1 What we check before saying yes
-----------------------------------
### TARIFF CONTEXT
Tariff drives the business case. We check what the client currently pays
per kWh including network, demand, and Time-of-Use components. The
higher the tariff and the larger the daytime consumption, the better the
case.
### ENERGY PROFILE
-   Twelve months of utility bills covering all relevant tariff
    structures and seasons.
-   Where available, 15-minute interval data from AMI metering --- not
    bill totals --- to understand load shape and peak demand.
-   Operating hours: a 24/7 cold-chain plant has a fundamentally
    different solar fit from a Mon--Fri 06:00--18:00 manufacturer.
### SITE SUITABILITY
-   Desktop check on roof area, orientation, obvious shading, and
    access. Google Maps and a phone call, not a site visit.
-   Red flags: asbestos sheeting, no usable roof area, no LV space, no
    decision-maker, multi-tenant complexity not yet resolved.
### COMMERCIAL APPETITE
-   What funding routes is the client open to: cash purchase, debt
    finance, Fedgroup-funded ROPO/lease, third-party PPA, or hybrid?
-   Who is the decision-maker, and on what timeline? If there is no
    decision-maker, there is no opportunity.
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **WHY WE QUALIFY HARD BEFORE DESIGNING**                             |
|                                                                      |
| Engineering effort is real cost. Spending design hours on an         |
| opportunity that was never serious is the cheapest way to lose money |
| in this business.                                                    |
|                                                                      |
| If First Assessment cannot be completed because the client will not  |
| provide bills or access, that is itself a Decline reason. Document   |
| and move on.                                                         |

Lifecycle Stage 2: Cost Proposal & Design
=========================================
*Concept-level engineering and a costed, decision-grade proposal. This
is where deals get real or die. Keep proposal-level design separate from
IFC design --- mixing them is how scope creep gets a company car.*
  **Stage**         Cost Proposal & Design
  ----------------- -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Purpose**       Produce a costed, technically defensible proposal with explicit assumptions, exclusions, and red flags.
  **Inputs**        First Assessment outputs, site visit, structural pre-screen, electrical pre-screen, tariff model, commercial structure preferences.
  **Exit gate**     Proposal issued and either accepted (move to Financial Close) or lost (close out).
  **Accountable**   Head of Project Development.
  **Responsible**   Project Developer + responsible Engineer.
  **Consulted**     Construction Manager (buildability), Finance (margin and FX), Compliance (regulatory route).
  **Tools**         Helioscope and PV\*SOL/PVsyst as applicable, SharePoint opportunity folder, app opportunity record, finance model.
  **Output**        Issued cost proposal with version-controlled feasibility pack.
2.1 Energy audit (concept level)
--------------------------------
-   Analyse utility bills: total kWh consumed, peak kVA demand, ToU
    periods (peak / standard / off-peak), seasonal variation.
-   Map the existing electrical single-line diagram (SLD) to a level
    sufficient to identify the likely point of common coupling and major
    constraints.
-   Identify operational hours and weekend light-load risk --- weekend
    daytime is the worst case for voltage rise on weak feeders.
2.2 Solar resource and shading
------------------------------
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **EMERGENT TOOLING RULE**                                            |
|                                                                      |
| Emergent uses Helioscope and PV\*SOL/PVsyst as applicable.           |
|                                                                      |
| The selected model output must be saved to SharePoint and linked to  |
| the project record in the app.                                       |
|                                                                      |
| The model version used for the cost proposal must be traceable. If   |
| the proposal price changes, the model that priced it must be         |
| retrievable.                                                         |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
-   Near shading (parapets, HVAC, water towers, neighbouring buildings)
    and horizon shading both modelled.
-   Partial shading on a single module disproportionately reduces the
    entire string output. This determines whether string inverters with
    multiple MPPT, microinverters, or DC optimisers are warranted ---
    call it explicitly in the proposal.
-   At least 10-year TMY data from a recognised source (PVGIS,
    SolarAnywhere, SASA-derived) calibrated for the site.
2.3 Structural assessment (concept level)
-----------------------------------------
Structural risk is the single biggest source of project delays and cost
overruns in C&I solar. We treat it that way.
-   A Professional Structural Engineer (PrEng or registered
    technologist) assesses roof structural integrity for combined dead
    load (panels + racking, typically 12--18 kg/m²) plus dynamic wind
    uplift loads.
-   Engineer checks: purlin spacing and gauge, sheet condition and
    gauge, portal frame or truss member sizes, existing point loads,
    connection details.
-   3D structural model with combined load case (original dead + live +
    new PV dead + wind uplift). Ground-mount triggers a geotechnical
    investigation.
-   Flat roofs prefer ballasted systems; where capacity is marginal,
    penetrating systems with fewer anchor points and longer rail spans
    may reduce point loads.
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **STAGE GATE LOGIC --- STRUCTURAL**                                  |
|                                                                      |
| If structural capacity is unknown or conditional, the proposal must  |
| show the assumption clearly.                                         |
|                                                                      |
| If reinforcement is required, it must be costed in scope or excluded |
| explicitly. There is no third option.                                |
|                                                                      |
| Owner: Engineering. Consulted: PM/Construction Manager. Approval for |
| high-risk exclusions: Head of Engineering or COO.                    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
2.4 Electrical infrastructure assessment (concept level)
--------------------------------------------------------
-   Existing MV/LV transformer capacity check --- can the transformer
    accept the proposed generation without reverse-power protection
    issues?
-   Identify MDB, sub-DBs, and the likely point of common coupling for
    inverter AC connection.
-   Existing CoC status check --- a supplementary CoC for a solar
    addition cannot be issued if the existing installation has no valid
    CoC.
-   If inverter capacity will exceed 350 kVA, a formal Grid Impact Study
    (GIS) is mandatory before municipal SSEG approval. Above 1 MVA,
    NERSA registration or licence/exemption is required.
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **EXISTING COC MUST BECOME A TRACKED RISK**                          |
|                                                                      |
| Existing CoC status is a tracked risk item from this stage onward,   |
| not a Stage 6 surprise.                                              |
|                                                                      |
| If the existing installation is non-compliant, the proposal must     |
| define whether remediation is included, excluded, or client          |
| responsibility --- and the PM must not be ambushed at handover.      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
2.5 System sizing (concept level)
---------------------------------
-   DC array sizing based on the client\'s daytime offset target
    (typically 70--100% of daytime load). DC:AC ratios of 1.1--1.3 are
    common to lift capacity factor without overloading inverters.
-   Inverter strategy: string inverters dominate; central inverters for
    very large systems. Strings sized for min/max voltage at temperature
    extremes, MPPT current limits, and MPPT channel count.
-   Module layout optimised for roof area, orientation, tilt (5°--15° on
    flat industrial roofs balances yield and uplift), and row-to-row
    shading.
-   BESS sized to the load-shift window and discharge rate (C-rate),
    driven by demand reduction, peak shaving, time-of-use arbitrage, or
    backup objectives.

2.7 Tax incentives
------------------
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **TAX WORDING IS A CLIENT-SIDE CONSIDERATION, NOT AN EMERGENT        |
| OPERATIONAL PROMISE**                                                |
|                                                                      |
| Sections 12B and 12BA of the Income Tax Act provide accelerated      |
| depreciation for qualifying renewable energy assets. We may          |
| reference these to inform the client\'s evaluation.                  |
|                                                                      |
| We do not warrant tax outcomes. Tax language in a proposal is a      |
| client tax consideration to be confirmed by the client\'s tax        |
| adviser. Owner: PD/Finance to flag, client to confirm with their     |
| accountant.                                                          |
|                                                                      |
| Do not let tax wording become an accidental warranty.                |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
-   Section 12B (permanent): three-year accelerated depreciation
    (50/30/20) for renewable energy assets above 1 MW; full first-year
    deduction for PV systems not exceeding 1 MW.
-   Section 12BA (temporary, applicable for assets brought into use
    March 2023 to February 2025): 125% upfront deduction on qualifying
    assets --- confirm cut-off applicability for any project quoting it.
-   Qualifying assets typically include panels, inverters, mounting
    structures, and cabling directly related to generation. Confirm with
    the client\'s adviser before relying on it.

3.2 FX and procurement exposure
-------------------------------
South Africa\'s C&I solar supply chain is import-heavy. Modules and
inverters are USD- or EUR-denominated. The window between proposal and
PO is a real risk window.
-   FX exposure must be reviewed before the budget baseline is locked.
    If pricing is not locked with the supplier, the risk must be
    explicit in proposal assumptions and project margin risk.
-   Long-lead container shipping (typical 8--12 weeks ex-China) means PO
    timing affects revenue recognition. Procurement exposure feeds the
    FC pack.
-   Where pricing cannot be locked at FC, the funding route and client
    agreement must permit price adjustment, or Emergent carries the risk
    knowingly.

4.2 Regulatory approvals
------------------------
Most regulatory work sits in Planning, with closure under Compliance
Handover. Regulatory work may start earlier (e.g., GIS scoping in Stage
2), but stage ownership and evidence must be clear.
### SSEG APPLICATION --- EIGHT STEPS
The SAPVIA-documented Small-Scale Embedded Generation (SSEG) approval
process used by South African municipalities and Eskom:
-   1\. Application & Notification --- submit application forms, SLD,
    specifications, inverter datasheets, PrEng-signed design.
-   2\. Pre-Approval document review --- municipality checks
    completeness; expect municipality-specific forms (City of Cape Town,
    Tshwane, eThekwini, Ekurhuleni differ).
-   3\. Grid Impact Study (≥350 kVA) --- client bears cost; allow 3--6
    weeks.
-   4\. Installation --- only after written pre-approval
    (approval-in-principle or signed-off approval letter).
-   5\. Testing & Commissioning --- system tested per commissioning
    checklist.
-   6\. Final Approval --- submission of as-built drawings, CoC, PrEng
    sign-off, commissioning report.
-   7\. Grid Connection --- municipality installs or upgrades the meter
    (often a bidirectional smart meter).
-   8\. Feed-in --- own-use confirmed and (where allowed) the feed-in or
    net-metering arrangement enabled.
Where the supply is from Eskom and the system exceeds 100 kVA, the Grid
Access Unit acts as the approval authority. Smaller systems still
require registration with the relevant network service provider.

### CERTIFICATE OF COMPLIANCE (COC)
-   CoC is the legal proof that the installation meets SANS 10142-1.
-   Only a registered Installation Electrician (IE) or Master
    Installation Electrician (MIE) may issue a CoC for a commercial PV
    installation. ETSP cannot issue for three-phase commercial systems.
-   A supplementary solar CoC requires a valid existing CoC for the
    property. The position on the existing CoC must be confirmed in
    Planning, not discovered at Commissioning.
### BUILDING PLAN SUBMISSION
-   In most municipalities, rooftop solar PV is a building work
    alteration requiring a building plan or rooftop layout submission
    and a signed structural assessment.
-   Building plan approval is a separate workstream from electrical
    SSEG; allow 4--8 weeks.
### NERSA
-   Systems below 1 MVA: registered through the municipality; no
    separate NERSA generation licence.
-   Systems 1 MVA and above: NERSA generation licence or exemption
    letter required before the municipality will consider the
    application.
-   Failure to register means the system operates illegally ---
    invalidating insurance and exposing the owner to fines. Compliance
    owns the registration evidence at handover.
4.3 Procurement and finance controls
------------------------------------
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **PROCUREMENT IS FINANCE, NOT JUST LOGISTICS**                       |
|                                                                      |
| Every procurement action connects to: approved budget line, PO,      |
| supplier, lead time, substitution approval if applicable, and        |
| SharePoint evidence.                                                 |
|                                                                      |
| An invoice without a PO is a red flag.                               |
|                                                                      |
| COS is realised only when BOTH conditions are met: an invoice is     |
| captured against the line AND the invoice-date cell colour on the    |
| workbook line is BLACK (the confirmed-actual signal). Either         |
| condition alone is not enough. Revenue realisation is then derived   |
| from realised COS via the cost-to-cost ratio                         |
| (revenueRealised\_line = (actualCOS\_line / totalCOScosted\_project) |
| × totalRevenueCosted\_project). Cash flow forecasting depends on     |

### PROCUREMENT RISKS TO MANAGE ACTIVELY
-   Port congestion at Cape Town and Durban can add 2--4 weeks to
    shipping timelines. Build buffer into the construction programme.
-   Lock pricing at contract signature where possible. PO-to-delivery on
    a 40ft container ex-China is typically 8--12 weeks.
-   Pre-shipment inspection (SGS, Bureau Veritas) is sensible for large
    module orders.
-   Hold float stock of fast-moving consumables (MC4 connectors, cable
    ties, earth clamps, small MCBs) to avoid on-site stoppages.

### HIGH-RISK ACTIVITIES TO MANAGE ACTIVELY
-   Working at heights --- IBR/corrugated or flat concrete roofs. Fall
    arrest, edge protection, safety nets per site-specific risk
    assessment. Scaffolding compliant with SANS 10085.
-   Lifting operations --- panel lifters (e.g., GEDA-class) for batch
    transport to roof. Manual chain-and-pallet methods cause breakages
    and injuries.
-   Electrical work --- all live work under LOTO. The existing
    installation remains live during most of the project.
-   Heat stress --- South African industrial roofs exceed 60°C in
    summer. Rotational shifts, shaded rest areas, hydration protocols
    enforced.
-   Panel handling --- modules are 20--25 kg and fragile; breakages
    don\'t just cost hardware, they delay the programme waiting for
    replacements.

5.4 Civil works (where applicable)
----------------------------------
-   Pile-driving or concrete-pad foundations for ground-mount.
-   Cable trenches: typically 600--900 mm deep, sand-bedded, warning
    tape above; SANS 10400 compliance for proximity to other services.
-   Access roads and hardstanding for inverter and transformer pads.
-   Cable ladder and trunking from roof penetrations to MDB.
-   Civil contractors must be BCCEI-registered and accepted under our
    HSE process.
5.5 Mounting structure installation
-----------------------------------
-   IBR/corrugated metal roofs --- penetrative hooks fixed to purlins
    with stainless or galvanised fasteners and EPDM seals; rails
    parallel or perpendicular to ridge per design.
-   Flat concrete or membrane --- ballasted (no penetrations) where
    structural capacity allows, or mechanical anchors. Wind uplift per
    SANS 10160-3 and the project-specific structural sign-off.
-   Ground mount --- driven steel piles where soil allows; horizontal
    rails connect rows.
-   All metal rails and module frames bonded to the earth system using
    minimum 6 mm² copper earth conductors per SANS 10142-1.
5.6 Module installation --- QA hold points
------------------------------------------
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| **QA HOLD POINTS ARE SIGNED, NOT HOPED FOR**                         |
|                                                                      |
| Module installation has hard QA hold points: torque records, photo   |
| evidence, sample inspections, NCR process, and sign-off by Quality / |
| Construction Manager.                                                |
|                                                                      |
| Do not rely on \'installer says done\'. That is not a control; it is |
| hope wearing a hard hat.                                             |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
-   Modules transported to roof in batches via panel lifter; never
    dragged. Scratched backsheets void warranties.
-   Mid-clamps and end-clamps torqued to manufacturer-specified value
    with a calibrated torque wrench. Torque records retained per
    row/area.
-   String design respects min/max Voc at temperature extremes.
    Mismatched MC4 connectors are forbidden --- even visually compatible
    connectors from different manufacturers can arc and cause fires.
5.7 DC cabling
--------------
-   DC string cables (typically 4 mm² or 6 mm² H1Z2Z2-K solar cable, UV
    and temperature rated) routed in cable trays or conduit from panels
    to inverters.
-   Cables labelled at both ends --- string number, polarity.
-   DC insulation resistance tests (up to 1,000 V DC) before connecting
    to inverters.
-   DC clamp meter check on each string --- significant deviations from
    expected Isc indicate wiring errors or module faults.
-   Combiner boxes (where used): string-level fuses and a common DC
    breaker before the inverter.
5.8 Inverter installation
-------------------------
-   Wall- or floor-mounted; inside or in weatherproof enclosures
    outside. Ventilation per manufacturer --- derating in hot
    environments is a real performance risk.
-   DC connections per string channel; AC connections to dedicated MCBs
    in the MDB.
-   Earth continuity from inverter chassis to site earth electrode
    verified.
-   AC and DC SPDs (Type 1+2 or Type 2) per SANS 10142-1-2.
5.9 AC grid connection
----------------------
-   Dedicated solar AC generation MCB or MCCB in the MDB.
-   Lockable AC isolator (visible break) adjacent to the inverter ---
    utility must be able to isolate the generator from the grid side.
-   Bidirectional energy meter (supplied and installed by the
    municipality). Meter installation is consistently the last-mile
    bottleneck --- work it weekly.
-   Anti-islanding protection configured and tested. Passive
    (frequency/voltage) and active methods both active by default.

6.2 Pre-commissioning (\'cold\') checks
---------------------------------------
-   Visual inspection of all mechanical installations.
-   Torque verification on module clamps and cable lugs.
-   Earth continuity tests on all bonded metalwork.
-   DC insulation resistance per string (string off; IR from + to earth
    and -- to earth; minimum 1 MΩ).
-   String open-circuit voltage (Voc) measurement; compared against
    calculated Voc corrected for ambient temperature.
-   AC cable insulation resistance test.
-   Inverter configuration verified: grid parameters (voltage,
    frequency, response times) per SSEG and municipality.
6.3 Live commissioning tests
----------------------------
-   String current at operating temperature; compared to expected Isc
    derated for irradiance.
-   Inverter MPPT tracking verified.
-   Anti-islanding test --- simulate grid loss; confirm disconnection
    within required time (typically \<0.2 s for loss-of-mains).
-   Protection relay tests --- under/over voltage and frequency trips.
-   Export-limiting verification where required by the municipality.
-   Monitoring system commissioning --- inverter logging, cloud
    connection, data streaming confirmed.
-   Performance check --- at known irradiance, measured output vs.
    modelled output. \>95% performance ratio is the target benchmark.

7.1 Why O&M is non-negotiable
-----------------------------
Solar becomes a long-term asset only when maintained. A 1 MWp system
losing 5% to soiling and undetected faults represents hundreds of
thousands of Rands in lost generation per year over a 20-year life.
Systems that are not actively monitored degrade silently, and the client
will eventually blame the installer for a 30%-underperforming asset that
could have been corrected in week 2.

7.4 Common corrective faults to expect
--------------------------------------
-   Shaded or dirty modules --- picked up on thermal imaging and
    monitoring deviations.
-   Failed strings --- MC4 connector failures, rodent damage, module
    bypass-diode failure.
-   Inverter faults --- capacitor degradation, fan failures, IGBT
    failures. Distributor lead times of 2--4 weeks are common; plan
    accordingly.
-   DC earth faults --- systematic string isolation and IR testing
    required to locate.
-   Anti-islanding nuisance trips --- weak municipal feeders cause
    inverter trips on voltage excursions; address by tuning protection
    within SSEG-permitted limits.
-   Metering disputes --- read errors or tariff changes affecting
    billing; reviewed monthly against monitoring data.

Hidden Operational Complexities
===============================
*These don\'t appear in marketing brochures but determine whether a
project is profitable or a nightmare. The failure-mode register is the
primary control; this section is the narrative.*
Existing infrastructure surprises
---------------------------------
Brownfield C&I sites --- cold-chain, food processing, heavy industry ---
require deeper upfront investigation than the marketing assumes. Ageing
distribution boards, undocumented cabling, and operational constraints
are normal, not exceptional. Typical shocks we have seen:
-   Transformer at or near capacity --- cannot accept solar generation
    without a costly upgrade.
-   Existing distribution boards non-compliant --- no valid CoC,
    outdated protection, illegal connections.
-   Roof condition worse than the site visit suggested --- hidden
    corrosion, structural failures.
-   Asbestos roof sheeting on pre-1990 industrial buildings --- triggers
    a separate asbestos removal regulatory process before any roof work.
Multi-tenant / sectional title
------------------------------
Sectional title industrial parks need body corporate or landlord
approval, shared metering agreements, and sometimes individual SSEG
applications per tenant meter. Add weeks to months for legal and
administrative work, and surface this in Stage 1, not Stage 4.
Generator integration
---------------------
Many C&I sites run diesel backup generators. Adding solar requires
careful protection design to prevent solar islanding onto the generator
busbar --- destroying both the generator and the inverters. Where BESS
is included, the controls must arbitrate the priority sequence cleanly.
The Functional Description for any BESS must be in the IFC pack.
Loadshedding and backup
-----------------------
A standard grid-tied solar inverter shuts down on grid loss
(anti-islanding). To deliver backup during loadshedding, either:
-   A hybrid inverter with battery system is required, or
-   An automatic transfer switch isolates a critical-load sub-board,
    with solar and battery providing reference, while the rest of the
    site is disconnected from the grid.
This is a key upsell opportunity and a complex electrical design
exercise. Treat it as a Stage 2 conversation, not a Stage 5 retrofit.
Metering reality
----------------
-   Some municipalities pre-approve specific meter models before
    installation.
-   Waiting lists for meter installation can be 3--6 months after
    Commissioning.
-   Some apply restrictive export limits (zero export \'no feed-in\')
    requiring export-limiting hardware.
-   SSEG tariff schedules can change mid-project; the financial model
    must be stress-tested.

Critical Success Factors
========================
*Repeated here so they are next to each other when the playbook is read
end-to-end.*
How we win
----------
-   Invest in pre-engineering. Stage 2 done well prevents the
    structural, electrical, and regulatory surprises that destroy
    project margins.
-   Lock procurement early. Order at contract signature, not after every
    approval. Parallel-path approvals and procurement saves 6--8 weeks.
-   Use SAPVIA-accredited installers and registered professionals.
    First-time approval rates are dramatically higher.
-   Manage the municipality relationship actively. Assign one person to
    chase every approval step. The approval process does not
    self-manage.
-   Don\'t skip commissioning tests. Unverified or incorrectly
    commissioned systems cause voltage rise, islanding, and harmonic
    distortion --- threatening grid stability and the client\'s SSEG
    approval.
-   Build a monitoring-first O&M culture from day one. Systems that are
    not actively monitored degrade silently.
-   Integrate HSE from the PD-to-PM handover. A safety incident does
    more than harm people: it stops the site, can invalidate cover, and
    creates personal criminal exposure for appointed persons under South
    Africa\'s OHS Act.
How we lose
-----------
-   Designing on incomplete bills, then re-pricing after award.
-   Promising tax outcomes the client\'s accountant later disputes.
-   Letting the PD remain operational owner after FC.
-   Allowing component substitutions to happen silently, then
    discovering the SSEG approval is invalid.
-   Treating commissioning as \'switching it on\'.
-   Treating O&M handover as paperwork.
-   Treating Hold as a stage instead of a status.
